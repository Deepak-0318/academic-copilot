- Develop proficiency in designing efficient algorithms to solve complex computational problems across diverse domains using techniques like divide-and-conquer, greedy methods, and backtracking.
- Gain the ability to evaluate and compare algorithm performance through rigorous analysis, including time and space complexity, to optimize solutions for practical applications.
- Master the implementation of fundamental algorithms, such as sorting, searching, and graph traversal, in a programming environment to build robust software solutions.
- Cultivate problem-solving skills by applying algorithmic strategies and data structures to real-world challenges, fostering innovation in areas like optimization, networking, and data processing.

## Syllabus:

## Module - 1 Introduction to Algorithms, Basics of Searching and Sorting

No. of hours

12

Introduction to Algorithms, Asymptotic notation and growth of functions, Analyzing complexity of algorithms, Recurrence relation, Substitution method, Recursion Tree Method, Master's Theorem, Brute force Design Technique : Linear Search, Naïve string-matching algorithm. Sorting methods: Bubble sort, Insertion Sort, and Selection Sort.

## Module - 2 Divide and Conquer &amp; Decrease and Conquer

No. of hours

8

Divide &amp; Conquer: Merge Sort, Quick-Sort, Strassen's Matrix Multiplication Decrease &amp; Conquer: Binary Search, Heap sort, Basics of graphs : Graph representation, DFS and BFS, Counting sort.

| Module - 3 G reedy Design Technique | No. of hours |
|---------------------------------------|----------------|

an initiative of RV EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS

Elements of greedy strategy, Knapsack problem, Job sequencing with deadlines, Huffman encoding. Spanning trees, Minimum cost spanning trees: Kruskal's and Prim's algorithms; Single source shortest path algorithms, Dijkstra's Algorithms.

## Module - 4 Dynamic Programming

No. of hours

8

Elements of dynamic programming, illustration of use of dynamic programming with specific examples such as 0/1 Knapsack problem, Matrix chain multiplication, finding longest common subsequence, Bellman-Ford Algorithm, Floyd Warshal's algorithm.

## Module - 5 String Matching and NP Completeness

No. of hours

7

Backtracking: N-Queens problem, Hamiltonian Cycle. Branch &amp; Bound: Knapsack Problem

String matching: Rabin-Karp algorithm, Knuth-Morris-Pratt algorithm.

Introduction of NP-completeness: P, NP, NPC, NPH problems.
