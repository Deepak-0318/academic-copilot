## Course Objectives:

- a) To review fundamental concepts of probability theory like probability spaces, sample space, relative frequency, conditional probability, Bayes' Theorem, independence of events.
- b) Discuss discrete random processes described by discrete random variables and illustrate different pmf like Geometric, Binomial, Multinomial and Poisson distributions and calculate useful quantities like expectations, moments, variance of a sum, correlation coefficient,
- c) Discuss random processes described by continuous random variables and calculate probabilities and expectations using cdf and pdf.
- d) Describe regression and CLT to be used in sampling-based hypothesis testing including z-score, critical value, p-value, t-test and for illustrate chi square goodness of fit.

## Syllabus:

## Module -1: Probability Basics

No. of hours

8

Probability spaces, sample space, relative frequency, conditional probability, Bayes' Theorem, independence of events. Simple random walk (one dimensional, two dimensional), distribution of the fortune, Gambler's ruin problem.

## Module -2: Discrete Distributions

No. of hours

12

Discrete random variables - Independent random variables; Bernoulli trials, Geometric, Binomial, Multinomial and Poisson distributions; Poisson approximation to the binomial distribution, joint distributions, sums of independent random variables, expectation of discrete random variables, moments, variance of a sum, correlation coefficient, Chebyshev's Inequality.

| Module - 3: Continuous Distributions | No. of hours |
|----------------------------------------|----------------|
| | 12 |

Continuous random variables- properties, cumulative and density distribution functionsnormal, exponential and gamma densities. Bivariate distributions and their properties; distribution of sums and quotients; conditional densities and expectations (Bayes ' rule).

## Module -4: Regression

No. of hours

5

Curve fitting by the method of least squares- fitting of straight lines, second degree parabolas; correlation and regression - Rank correlation (Spearman and Pearson).

## Module -5: Hypothesis Testing

No. of hours

8

Sampling distribution, Central Limit Theorem, null hypothesis, alternate hypothesis, zscore, critical value, p-value, t-test; Chi-square test for goodness of fit and independence of attributes.

## Course outcomes: After completing the course, students will be able to:

- a) CO1: Apply discrete probability distribution functions to solve real world problems.
- b) CO2: Apply continuous probability distribution functions to solve real world problems.
- c) CO3: Apply the method of least squares to fit a data set, and interpret the results in the context of the problem at hand.
- d) CO4: Test a given hypothesis using methods like critical value, p-value and chi-square tests for goodness of fit and independence of attributes.
